Into the Eagle's Nest

The castle's four floors, each drawn from the kilobyte of description the tape carries for it.

01 · The floors

Four floors, 128 tiles square

The whole of each floor, drawn one pixel a tile from its description and the table of blocks.

Floor
open floor and nothing wall a figure, a pickup or a door

Each floor is 32 rows of 32 bytes in the memory under the input and output chips, and each byte names one of the blocks at $7000, four tiles across and four down. That makes a map of 128 tiles each way, which the game builds at $9000 when the player arrives on the floor. The screen shows 28 tiles by 21 of it at a time.

The pictures above are drawn the way the builder at $6A4B builds the map, and then coloured a pixel a tile from the tile colour table at $8600. The walls stand out because they are the only thing in the castle drawn in cyan.

02 · What a floor holds

The figures, the charge and the prisoner

Eight bytes of a floor's description are not blocks at all.

FloorBlocks namedFiguresChargePrisoner

Counted from the four descriptions in the listing. A figure's code is $30 to $37, two for each of the four kinds; the charge is $62 and the prisoner $63.

The builder picks those bytes out as it goes. Every figure it finds is added to a list in $8100 to $8500, which holds each one's place in the map and its kind, and the figures then take turns one a frame. The charge and the prisoner each have one place kept for them, and the charge is only put on the map at all when the demolition mission is being played and that floor's charge has not yet been set off.
