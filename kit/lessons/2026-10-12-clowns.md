## next · 2026-10-12 · Clowns (C64) · Vai

- **A cartridge that holds GAME low is analysed from its `.crt`, and the
  video chip reads its ROM.** Clowns is an Ultimax cartridge: its 8 KB sit
  where the KERNAL was, the snapshot's RAM image holds only the power-on
  pattern there, and the font and two sprites the screen shows are read by
  the video chip straight from the ROM's top 4 KB. The listing was built
  from the `.crt`, and a frame drawn with those 4 KB in the video chip's
  view matched the emulator to the pixel; without them it drew blank. The
  emulator's own call for the video chip's state answered with RAM while
  the cartridge was in, which stopped the frame recorder until it read the
  chip through the I/O bank. `c64-reference`, "Ultimax cartridges", holds
  the measurements.
- **Code compiled from a high-level language has a calling convention to
  find first.** Half of Clowns opens every procedure with one call that
  builds a frame on a stack of its own and passes its arguments past the
  caller's frame, keeps its globals in the main program's frame, prints
  text from the bytes after a call, and reaches the machine code only by
  number through one table. Reading those few routines first turned the
  rest into a list of procedures with named arguments, and the table gave
  the trace 1.4 KB of code no instruction referred to.
- **The comments that were wrong were about registers and modes, not
  purpose.** A sample of 60 found 9 wrong: which register a routine
  returns its result in, a constant table read off by one entry, and text
  said to appear that the attract mode never shows. Each was a detail
  written from the call site in front of the annotator rather than from
  the routine's end, and from the game rather than from the attract mode
  that runs the same code with its flags cleared.
